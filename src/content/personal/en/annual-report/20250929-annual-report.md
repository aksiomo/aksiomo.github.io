## Before I begin

Today is my birthday, an ordinary Monday. In a gap at work, I wrote this piece.

Without my noticing, I am already 24. Time flies.

Looking across the world now, nationalism is growing as never before, and internationalism grows weaker by the day. As an idealist I carry a desire I have no way to speak, and an ideal with nowhere to set it down. My own ambitions, and my view of where society is going, are both a grey haze.

But what gets done depends on the people who do it. I still want to walk this whole world, to look, and to live it.

I think of the words of a banned song by Beyond:

> To live, one must revolt
> Heart high, spirit proud
> Where is there that we cannot go?

## The year

As the epigraph says, I met my ordinary twenty-fourth year on an ordinary day. In the body of this piece I want to write what the year was like, and at the same time try to take "myself" apart and lay the inside out in detail. After that, I want to write what I intend for the future.

The year I was 23 had too many turns. After I came back from Harbin, pressed by how hard it was to find work, I stayed at home for half a year, kept by my family, and now and then took contract jobs. For half a year the argument inside me did not stop. On one side I watched the social questions that were hottest at the moment. At home and abroad, opposition, contradiction, and conflict did not let up. Bent under this huge current of the age, it was no longer possible to keep only myself clean. On the other side I began to see that the conflict between me and my family was probably already brewing. A wide gap in communication, born from the natural split between value-rationality and instrumental rationality, and from different levels of understanding, was taking shape day by day. I understood that if I could not stand on my own economically, I could hardly win a right to speak, and I could hardly stand as a person. If I settled for things as they were, I would have no future. I quickened my pace. Not long after, I found the work I had hoped for, and at last got out of that bind at the level of money. But even if income can give a first independence, my present skill and my present understanding are far from enough to defend the modern life I want. I can say it without hedging: up to now I am still a giant in thought and a dwarf in action. I am still very far from the goal.

The clash of views I mentioned is not only with the people closest to me. Even among people of my own age and generation, there are plenty whose way is different and with whom I dare not force a fit. (One thing is certain. Friends who have read this far: there must be some place where we are alike.) Many friends I once talked with about everything have slowly grown distant. The struggle over subjectivity has made me afraid, and tired of it. The people I rarely crossed paths with before are the ones who still keep a friendship with me that is neither warm nor cold. I did not expect that. Friendship is already so precious to me, and I still dare to long for a love I have never had in twenty-three years. That is as ironic as it gets.

I am someone who badly lacks a sense of being recognized. In the language of psychoanalysis, I have a typical hysterical personality: I want to be desired by the Big Other. People who know me, if they recall how I have acted, can arrive at roughly the same answer this language gives. By definition I cannot live for myself. Advice like "love yourself before you love another" cannot do its work on me. I live, by nature, inside other people's judgments, inside patterns of behavior that are close to a performance. In a society that grows more atomized, where lives cross less and less, it is hard to say this is not a curse for someone who both longs to leave a conservative clan and can hardly enter the atomized peer groups of the city.

Without recognition from the Other, I lost self-recognition, and the ability to keep an identity the same. The plainest sign is that I create new accounts again and again, and step into new circles online. I thoroughly scourge the previous me — perhaps as I am doing now — add a pure performance aimed at the new group, and rebuild my sense of who I am. In that way I manage a kind of reincarnation of the spirit. To be honest, when an identity like that is first set up, I am extremely excited. Then, as time passes, when the new identity is struck, or simply is no longer watched by the Other, I rush to find a new one and throw the old one away. Most of the time this cycle is painful. I understand with complete clarity that even a new recognition will wear out with time, just as the last one did. This thought, and this behavior, are not things I can stop.

This is also why Kanto Luna was born. Not long ago I shaped this identity. It is no different from the ones before it, and it too was born, in some measure, to flatter the Other and to perform. But it was chaotic enough from the start. It takes those earlier selves as part of itself. It is not pure. So I do not have to avoid declaring it. Declaring it in the open can also bind it completely to "me", so that it can no longer be split off with ease. And so I became Kanto Luna, and Kanto Luna became me. This is a modern madman's attempt to save himself.

The taking-apart of the self, and the part about the remedy, ends here. It is already late as I write. The clock passed midnight long ago, and it is no longer my birthday. I will write briefly about how I look ahead. Even if, as the epigraph says, the future looks grey from here, I still want to write this part. A person ought to have at least something to hope for.

The future is what it has been: I need to work hard. I need to set my mind in order, and get out of fantasies of death. I need to give myself to the things I love doing.

I forgot to say this. Recently I joined the Chinese Esperanto League, and in the near future I will also sit the Esperanto proficiency exams. I think that after computers I have found another cause in which I can be needed, into which I can pour my desire, and to which I can give my short life.

That is enough. I wish myself a good dream.

To everyone who has read this far, whatever attitude and mood you brought to this piece — fellow feeling, or mockery — thank you.
