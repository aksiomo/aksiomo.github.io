From ancient times, autumn has been met with grief at the loneliness. Without my noticing, deep autumn is here again. If a life is seventy-five springs and autumns in all, then after this night one third of it will have passed.

In recent years, whenever this season comes, it is almost always a turn in the story, though the situations are never quite the same. The year before last I stayed in Harbin. The autumn wind was cold, but there was still heat in my chest. The circumstances were hard, and still I could imagine a future freely, and feel that in the vastness of heaven and earth only freedom is precious. Last year, work was for a while a small improvement. I was pleased with myself, and I lived a lavish stretch of spending money like dirt. Looking back now, I only want to beat my chest and stamp my feet. After that, things went downhill day by day. Today the autumn wind is bleak. I am full of ambition, and I am in a low tide again.

Even so, looking back over this year, it was not all decline. A living made at work has gone down like a river, but my relations with people have picked up. Before this I kept my distance from people. I came and went alone, and I stood apart. This year I have come to know quite a few friends, and I am fortunate to be close with several of them. That is rare. Tongtong is among them, of course. She is willing to choose, and to keep choosing, me — someone with nothing of value to his name — and she has always thought more highly of me than I do of myself. To be valued like this is a first in my life. I hardly know how to receive it.

Over this year, both temperament and will have been trained and tested a long way. I have changed a great deal. Perhaps without noticing I have become smoother, and more seasoned. I understand people's hearts more deeply, and I am no longer as foolish and one-sided as I was. Some things have not changed. The pursuit of idealism, and the longing that goes with it, and hard thinking about how things actually stand, will without doubt be the ground color carved on my tombstone.

In the year I was 24, the deepest thing I came to know is this: a person, as a social being, cannot truly exist alone. A person and other people, a person and the crowd, a person and society, are tied deeply together by an invisible net. Connection. Only connection. An atomized postmodernity has no future. I want to keep that end from coming. I will spare no effort in practicing those good premodern virtues: honesty, integrity, selflessness, and kindness. I will spare no effort in helping other people. I will be connected with them.
