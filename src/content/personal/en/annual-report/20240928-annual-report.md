## Before I begin

Time is the hardest thing to come by, and another spring and summer have gone. This birthday was especially cold and quiet — cold, and colder still. All I had in front of me was the monitor and a cake I bought for myself. The melancholy was vague and hard to name. Looking back, the days that are gone were lovely, and they will not return.

The day at work was heavy. By the time I got off it was already six or seven. I packed onto the subway, came back to a monotonous rented room, turned on the monitor, and set about writing in earnest. Just debugging the blog's responsive layout took a long time, and in the end it still failed, so I let it go.

I put on music — Wang Wen's "Shui Zhi Mei" — and typed. Thoughts crowded in. One moment they stuck in my throat and poured out all at once; the next I did not know where to begin. The rambling was inevitable. I write these words down as a keepsake.

## A stranger in another city

After graduation I chose to stay in Harbin for a while and build up a first store of assets. Classmates and friends had already scattered to the ends of the earth, and there is no longer anyone I know well in Harbin. After meals I sometimes go out for a walk. On the crowded overpasses I realized how large Harbin really is. The forest of steel and concrete seems to drink up a person's spirit and life by some magic. Standing on a bridge, facing the tracks that run out to the horizon, I often turn many things over. Drifting in a strange place, living alone, is lonelier and harder than I imagined. I no longer know what is holding me up. Maybe it is a refusal to compromise. Maybe it is the unwillingness to give up standing apart. One thing was settled long ago, and it was my first thought and my first dream: to walk to the end of the horizon, to measure every inch of this world with my own feet, to see for myself the different lands and ways of north and south. I still remember a line I wrote in a notebook in middle school: "unbound, and in love with freedom." It may have been childish, but I am afraid the thirst for freedom was cut into my bones very early. I chose this road myself, so there is no turning back. Even if I have to crawl, I will keep going.

Where is the next stop? I don't know, but it will not be under my feet. Maybe one day I will stop. When that day comes, the end of my soul will be close at hand.

## A dull life

Since graduation, the line between the company and the rented room has been the ordinary shape of life. Now and then I have the urge to walk the streets for a couple of turns, listen to the noise of the neighborhood, and watch people come and go. That is comfort enough. Harbin has a thick breath of ordinary life. It does not have that coldness of people who seem to have left the world behind. Outside the window of the rented room are two barbecue stalls. Every night I hear the noisy voices and do not find them tiresome at all. The noise lets me forget that I am on my own. Harbin has a warmth that answers its climate. It can give a person a little ease even inside an ice cave. I think I love this city, and the plain people who live in it, down to the bone.

On the days I do not go out, I am more often alone at home, facing a small 23-inch monitor. That little space has become my world, as if it were the whole of me. It walls me off from a deathlike loneliness and lets me look for a thread of comfort. Those made-up stories, those virtual worlds, those lovable people made of zeroes and ones have become the stars in the universe of my solitude. They bring a light that does not run out, so that even in exhaustion I can still see a little life. Those absurd, uncanny stories pull me in hard. They let me look over the edge of madness, sting my nerves like a drug, and drive the release of dopamine. The next morning I wake up, and the work repeats as usual.

## Feelings with nowhere to rest

Words I once treated as precious I can now say without a change of face, the way one bargains in a market. More than once I have fallen into an endless questioning of myself: I really am growing uglier. Made-up stories still move me, but every time I grow calm I understand that if I were put in the place of those protagonists, the story could not go on as written. Those dispositions are ones I can hardly have. Carrying that thought may still be enough to set my own conduct straight.

To let the emptiness stop eating at me, I can often only borrow music, and let the rushing melody run through my chest. Other works only make the torn hollow more violent.

## At the end

Enough of this. The night is deep and still, so I will stop here. "After calamity, one knows a knight's spirit; with a right heart, one then knows oneself." I will close with a passage from *Legend of Mortal*, as a word of encouragement to myself.

> "All of this has meaning.
> You do not speak. The clang of blades meeting is Zhao Huo's shout, ringing up to the clouds, meant to make everyone under heaven prick up their ears.
> Both of them are deep in the fight, and little by little they enter a state where self and world drop away.
> Without agreeing to, each remembers their own youth, the first wish that drew them toward the jianghu, hearing the same story and wanting to become a great hero.
> Unlike Rui Sheng, who was given every advantage — a talent not found in ten thousand, raised on Mount Guyun, never short of food or clothing, the sect's only disciple, taught everything his master had, certain to amount to something given time.
> You were born with nothing. You are covered in dust, and you stumble as you go. There is nowhere on you that is stronger than other people. To call you mediocre is already too kind. You were supposed to accept your fate, give up, live without distinction, and build nothing in the whole of your life.
> Yet the sect leader said: that is bullshit.
> Everyone has the right to dream. You rarely get to come and wander in this mortal world. Why not stay wild, and refuse to wake."
